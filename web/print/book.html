A Year of Robots

My robot activity book

This book belongs to

50 weeks · build, play, talk, level up · https://pandagardenio.github.io/robokids/web/

🧭 How this book works

  • One build session per week (30–45 min), plus one or two 10–15 minute play sessions.
  • Every week has the same shape: Build → Play → Talk about it → Level up → Robot Journal.
  • Kid leads, parent assists. They choose names, colors, rules and numbers. You handle batteries, wiring and loading the programs.
  • Weeks 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 are showcases with slack built in. Skip or repeat any week.
  • Track progress with the progress checklist.
  • Every week has two pages: the activity (for doing it together) and a Robot Journal page (for your kid to draw and write).
  • Programs (from week 11) are loaded on a laptop from the website. A grown-up does that part; the week's page says where.
  • Tick the box at the top of each week when it's done, and color a circle on the fridge poster.
🦺 Safety first. Coin cells (CR2032) are dangerous if swallowed: grown-ups hold them, tape them in and store them out of reach. Adults handle craft knives, hot glue and wire stripping.

Phase 1 · Circuits and Motors

LED Robot Eyes

~45 min build + short play sessions during the week · Skill: A circuit is a loop

Done!

🧰 You need

  • 2 LEDs (5 mm, any color)
  • 2 CR2032 coin cells
  • Masking tape
  • A cardboard robot head (any box)

🔧 Build together Together

  1. Slide the LED legs around the coin cell: long leg on the + side (the flat, printed side).
  2. It lights? Wrap it tightly in tape so the battery cannot fall out.
  3. Poke two holes in the box and push the lit LEDs through as eyes.

🎮 Play Kid time

Turn off the room lights and let the robot 'look around' the house. Flip one LED around: why does it stay dark?

💬 Talk about it Together

What has to touch what to make the light turn on?

⭐ Level up Kid time

Try different colors. Does a red LED and a blue LED look different in the dark?

📖 Words this week For grown-ups

Word What it means Say it to your kid
LED A tiny light. It only works one way round: the long leg goes to plus (+). A tiny light that only works the right way round.
Circuit A complete loop that electricity travels around: out of the battery, through the parts, and back in. Any gap stops everything. A round trip for electricity. If the path is broken, nothing works.
Coin cell (CR2032) A flat, round battery the size of a big button. Dangerous if swallowed: grown-ups handle it (see Safety). A flat battery. Only grown-ups hold it.
Plus (+) and minus (−) The two ends of a battery. Electricity leaves from one end and has to find its way back to the other. The battery's way out and way back in.

Paper Circuit Card

~45 min build + short play sessions during the week · Skill: Circuits on paper

Done!

🧰 You need

  • Copper tape (5 mm, conductive adhesive)
  • 1 LED
  • 1 CR2032 coin cell
  • Card stock
  • Binder clip

🔧 Build together Together

  1. Draw a robot on the card. Mark where the eye (LED) goes.
  2. Run two copper tape lines from the eye to a corner (don't let them touch).
  3. Bend the LED legs flat and tape them onto the lines; long leg on the + line.
  4. Put the coin cell in the corner, + side up, and fold the corner over it. Clip it.

🎮 Play Kid time

Give the glowing card to someone in the family. Can they figure out how it works?

💬 Talk about it Together

What happens if the two copper lines touch each other?

⭐ Level up Kid time

Add a second LED eye on the same two lines.

Two Eyes, Two Ways

~45 min build + short play sessions during the week · Skill: Series vs parallel

Done!

🧰 You need

  • 4 LEDs
  • 2 AA battery holder with switch + 2 AA batteries
  • Alligator clip leads (6)

🔧 Build together Together

  1. Connect two LEDs one after the other (series). Note how bright.
  2. Now connect them side by side (parallel). Note how bright.
  3. Compare: which robot eyes are brighter?

🎮 Play Kid time

Make a 'sleepy robot' (series, dim) and an 'awake robot' (parallel, bright) and act them out.

💬 Talk about it Together

Why did one way look dimmer?

⭐ Level up Kid time

Unscrew one LED in each version. What happens to the other one?

📖 Words this week For grown-ups

Word What it means Say it to your kid
Series Parts connected one after the other in a single line. They share the battery's push, so LEDs glow dimmer, and if one goes out they all do. Holding hands in a line: everyone shares.
Parallel Parts connected side by side, each with its own path to the battery. LEDs glow brighter, and one going out doesn't stop the others. Everyone gets their own path.
LED A tiny light. It only works one way round: the long leg goes to plus (+). A tiny light that only works the right way round.
Switch Something that opens or closes a gap in a circuit, to turn things off and on. A little door for electricity: open means off, closed means on.
Alligator clip A wire with a spring clip at each end, for connecting parts without tools. A wire with crocodile teeth.

Scribble Bot

~45 min build + short play sessions during the week · Skill: Unbalanced motors

Done!

🧰 You need

  • Paper cup
  • Small DC motor (3–6 V)

🔧 Build together Together

Cutebot mouse: your Cutebot. Optional: the SG90 servo from week 31 on S1 as a wagging tail.

DIY mouse - Kitronik Motor Driver Board for micro:bit (or similar) - 2 N20 micro gear motors (~100–300 RPM, 3–6 V) with 30–40 mm wheels - 1 small ball caster - 3×AAA battery holder with switch - 2 large paper clips, 3 jumper wires with crocodile clips, a small piece of stiff card for the base - Hot glue (parent)

🔧 Build: Cutebot mouse Together

  1. Cut a paper cup in half lengthwise and cover it in felt: that's the body.
  2. Glue on ears and googly eyes at the front, above the ultrasonic sensor (its two "eyes" stay uncovered).
  3. Tape the shell over the car, leaving the micro:bit screen visible as the mouse's "heart window".

🔧 Build: DIY mouse Together

  1. Base: cut a 7 × 11 cm card base, rounded at the front. Hot glue the two motors at the back, one on each side, shafts pointing out. Push the wheels on.
  2. Balance: glue the ball caster under the front. The mouse should sit level.
  3. Electronics: plug the micro:bit into the motor board and glue the board on top. Wire the left motor to Motor 1 and the right motor to Motor 2. Battery pack under or behind the board.
  4. Whiskers: bend each paper clip into a long, springy whisker and fix one to each front corner. Connect the left whisker to P1, the right to P2. Run a bare GND wire across the front, just behind both whiskers, so a bent whisker touches it. (Check your board's labels for where P1, P2 and GND are broken out; crocodile clips on the micro:bit's edge pads also work.)
  5. Test before decorating: load diy-mouse.ts with the Kitronik Motor Driver extension (see Load the programs). If the mouse spins instead of driving forward, swap one motor's two wires.

🎛️ Tuning knobs Together

  • whiskerCm (Cutebot): how close something must be before the mouse "feels" it.
  • SoundThreshold.Loud, 160: raise it if the mouse runs away from normal talking.
  • s > -48: how close counts as "found the cheese". Lower it (e.g. -55) if it never finds it.
  • lightLevel() < 15: how dark before it sleeps.

📖 Words this week For grown-ups

Word What it means Say it to your kid
Sensor A part that lets the robot notice the world: light, heat, distance, sound, movement. The robot's eyes, ears and skin.
Behavior One thing the robot does in response to something, like 'back away when it's loud'. Something the robot does when something happens.
Radio micro:bits can send short messages to each other through the air, no wires needed, across a house. Invisible messages through the air.
Signal strength How strong a radio message is when it arrives. Closer usually means stronger. Louder when close, quieter when far.
Whisker sensor A springy wire that touches another wire when it bends, closing a circuit the micro:bit can feel. A homemade touch sensor. When the whisker bumps something, the robot feels it.
Cutebot The robot car kit this project uses. The micro:bit slots in as its brain. Our robot car.
Chassis The base of a vehicle that everything else is attached to. The robot's skeleton.
Ultrasonic sensor The two round 'eyes' on the front of the Cutebot. They send out a sound too high to hear and time the echo to measure distance. The robot squeaks and listens for the echo, like a bat.
V2 The newer micro:bit, with a speaker, a microphone and a touch-sensitive logo. This project needs it. The newer micro:bit that can hear and talk.
Servo A motor that turns to an exact angle and holds still there, instead of spinning round and round. A motor that points instead of spins.
S1 A socket on the Cutebot labeled S1 where you plug in a servo. The servo's plug spot.
Motor driver board A board that gives motors enough power, with the micro:bit telling it what to do. The muscles helper for the motors.
Gear motor A motor with gears inside: slower but stronger, good for turning wheels. A slow, strong motor for wheels.
RPM Revolutions per minute: how many full turns a motor makes in one minute. How fast a wheel spins.
Volts (V) How hard a battery pushes electricity. One AA battery is 1.5 V; two make 3 V. How strong the battery's push is.
Ball caster A small ball in a holder that lets the front of a robot slide in any direction. A rolling ball foot.
Switch Something that opens or closes a gap in a circuit, to turn things off and on. A little door for electricity: open means off, closed means on.
Jumper wire A short wire for connecting parts, often with clips or pins on the ends. A short connecting wire.
Alligator clip A wire with a spring clip at each end, for connecting parts without tools. A wire with crocodile teeth.
Servo horn The small plastic arm that clips onto the servo and moves with it. The servo's little arm.
Shaft The metal rod that sticks out of a motor and spins. The motor's spinning nose.
Balance How weight is spread out. Low and heavy robots are harder to tip over. Low and heavy is hard to push over.
Pins (P1, P2, GND) The metal strips along the micro:bit's bottom edge. P1 and P2 can feel when they're connected to GND (the minus side). The micro:bit's fingers along the bottom.
